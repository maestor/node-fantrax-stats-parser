Updated 10 September 2026 using the refreshed roster exports and the clarified lineup/injury assumptions. All team names below refer to **FFHL fantasy teams**, whose players come from many NHL clubs. Ownership is frozen to the supplied rosters for the entire season, including the fantasy playoffs. Every statistic comes from NHL **regular-season** games; NHL playoffs never enter the model.

These are exploratory simulation probabilities, not calibrated betting-market odds. The model has not been backtested for probability accuracy. Decimal odds are `1 / championship probability`, including the returned stake and without a bookmaker margin. A dash means the model produces too few championships to quote a useful price. “<0.1%” is not a claim of impossibility. **What changed in this update.** The ownership snapshot still contains 2,149 players. Twenty-one team files contain player additions/removals, NHL-club updates or status changes. Most edits affect development depth or offseason lineup labels. All current numbers in this document have been regenerated.

The earlier model selected from the full owned pool, but its opportunity priors still disadvantaged some players marked Min and discounted fantasy IR labels. **Injuries and reserve depth.** Each team and scoring period has twelve sampled availability states. Before selecting the 23-player roster in each state, each skater has an 8% chance and each goalie a 6% chance of a full-period absence. Managers then choose from every owned player still available. Strong NHL reserves can replace the lost player; an unsigned prospect cannot supply the same immediate production. Within-period daily availability and the 12F/6D/1G limits still apply.

These absence rates are explicit modelling assumptions, not measured injury forecasts. Conditional availability is divided by the probability of avoiding the full-period absence, capped at 100%, to avoid simply subtracting the same missed games twice from historical workloads. Season-level volume/form uncertainty remains shared across periods. The full-period states are drawn independently: the model does not reproduce multiweek injury timelines or simulate emergency promotions as separate mid-period transactions.

Current NHL injury signals from the checked depth listings receive a further 25% workload discount, independently of fantasy status. This is a broad uncertainty allowance, not a predicted return date. Merzlikins is independently confirmed out indefinitely after shoulder surgery. [NHL report](https://www.nhl.com/news/elvis-merzlikins-out-indefinitely-for-columbus-after-surgery). The healthy sensitivity removes both that extra discount and the sampled full-period absences; historical missed games still influence its starting workload estimates.

**Rules implemented.** All categories have equal matchup weight.

| Skaters: 10 categories | Goalies: 5 categories |
|---|---|
| Goals, assists, points, plus/minus, PIM, shots on goal, power-play points, short-handed points, hits, blocks | Wins, saves, shutouts, goals-against average, save percentage |

Higher wins in every category except GAA, where lower wins. Plus/minus is compared as a signed value. Regular-season category results award 2/1/0 standings points. One team’s same period totals are compared against both opponents, preserving the shared good or bad week. Forty matchups give 600 category decisions and a maximum of 1,200 standings points per team; the league average is exactly 600 points.

The schedule contains every non-divisional opponent once, every divisional opponent twice, plus two distinct opponents from the other division of the same conference. That is `24 + 14 + 2 = 40` matchups. Simulated schedules have 20 periods, two distinct opponents in each period, and symmetric pairings. The unknown extra opponents and ordering are varied across simulations.

The playoffs use 16 teams, the NHL-style division/wild-card bracket, and four single fantasy matchups. Category ties count as half a category; a 7.5–7.5 matchup advances the team with more regular-season points. Equal regular-season points are resolved by a random priority because the next league tiebreak was not supplied. GAA and SV% require two goalie starts in the scoring period. One qualifying team wins both ratio categories against a non-qualifier; if neither qualifies, both ratio categories tie. The final is assumed to retain a two-start minimum over its full 13 days.

**Calendar assumption still needing the exact league dates.** The NHL regular season runs 29 September–10 April. [NHL announcement](https://www.nhl.com/news/nhl-announces-2026-27-regular-season-schedule). The baseline allocates 29 September–7 March into twenty equal eight-day proxy periods, then uses 8–14, 15–21 and 22–28 March for the first three playoff rounds, followed by the confirmed final. Thus every published NHL regular-season game belongs to exactly one fantasy period.

The eight-day periods approximate missing scoring-period boundaries; they are not claimed to be the actual Monday lock dates. The calendar sensitivity instead uses Monday-based weeks, combines the opening period with the following week, combines 21 December–3 January, and combines 1–14 February. These illustrative combinations also produce twenty regular periods. The exact FFHL calendar could change starts qualification, daily congestion, weekly selections and the final rankings.

**How the estimates were constructed.**

1. Parse all 32 `regular-2026-2027.csv` roster exports: 2,149 players, with no duplicate current Fantrax IDs. Keep ownership fixed. Link recent exports by Fantrax ID; use position and checked name aliases for NHL identity. Resolve same-name players separately—for example, the two Jack Hughes players are not combined.
2. Use official NHL regular-season summary and realtime data for 2023–24, 2024–25 and 2025–26. Fully paginate each response: skater counts are 924/920/940 and goalie counts 98/103/98. Map 1,076 owned players to an NHL history in this window; many remaining owners’ entries are prospects without NHL history. Historical fantasy CSV rates are a fallback for unmatched players with credited games, not a substitute for known NHL game/starts totals.
3. Estimate per-game skater rates from recency-weighted totals and games: weights 0.60/0.30/0.10, newest first. Shrink toward the position pool using eight prior games for common categories, 25 for plus/minus and 30 for short-handed points. Points are always goals plus assists. Players with no history receive 80% of the position-pool rate, with reduced playing opportunity.
4. Estimate NHL availability from 80% latest-season games and 20% prior-season games, divided by 82 historical NHL team games. Regress skater availability 15% toward 90%. Goalies use actual starts. For players without latest-season games, use 70% of prior-season games/starts, or 50% of the oldest season if needed; multiply by 1.0 for a checked NHL-roster role, 0.35 for contracted non-roster depth, or zero without identified opportunity. With no history in the three-year window, NHL-roster priors are 1.3 skater games or 0.3 goalie starts per representative week; contracted non-roster priors are 0.15 skater games or 0.03 starts. These deliberately conservative opportunity priors can miss successful prospects and returning veterans. The same rule applies to all fantasy statuses. Separately checked NHL injury signals reduce workload by 25%; missing NHL clubs receive no scheduled opportunities unless resolved.
5. For each of twelve sampled injury states per team and period, select 12F, 6D and one G, then four unrestricted bench players from the full owned pool. Use a category-value heuristic and NHL schedule, discounting bench value for daily slot competition. Sample daily skater availability 120 times within each state, cap appearances at 12F/6D, and calculate expected category totals. Stable player-based random draws keep unchanged rosters comparable across input snapshots. State-specific goalie selections are retained. One state is selected when drawing each fantasy period; its totals are shared against both opponents. Dual-eligible entries retain their exported primary position. Injury replacements at period boundaries are explicit; mid-period replacement is approximated through daily availability.
6. Simulate counting categories with Poisson draws, using a normal approximation above a mean of 25. Add shared volume and offensive-form variation so related offensive categories do not behave independently. Derive points from goals and assists; constrain PPP and SHP not to exceed total points. Plus/minus uses a signed normal draw with variance proportional to skater appearances.
7. Simulate goalie starts on actual scheduled NHL days. Within each fantasy roster, same-club goalies are mutually exclusive, and at most one goalie contributes per fantasy team per day. Select among announced starters by projected win rate. Draw shots faced and goals allowed; saves, shutouts, GAA and SV% all come from those same outcomes. Use 600 prior shots to stabilize goalie save percentage, and ten prior appearances for shot volume and win rate. Goalie appearances are approximated as 60 minutes; relief appearances and overtime minutes are not modelled. Ratios are calculated from aggregate saves/shots and goals/minutes, not averaged player percentages. Compare GAA rounded to two decimals and SV% to three.
8. Include persistent season-level uncertainty and fresh period-level form. Baseline lognormal standard deviations are 10% for skater volume, 12% for offence and 10% for peripheral rates; period variation adds 9% volume and 14% offence. Goalie save-percentage shifts have standard deviations 0.006 per season and 0.012 per period; workload has 12% season variation. These are modelling choices, not estimated confidence intervals. Shared season factors persist through the playoffs.
9. Run 20,000 baseline seasons and 10,000 per alternative with deterministic Mulberry32 seed `20260910`. Simulate standings, qualification, all four rounds and the extended final. The base run contains 12.8 million regular-season fantasy matchups and 300,000 playoff matchups.

The alternatives use: 85/10/5 recency weights; no checked-injury discount or sampled full-period absences; 40% more opportunity and 20% higher offensive rates for players with identified NHL opportunity and fewer than 20 recent NHL games, capped at full availability; wider season uncertainty of 18% volume/20% offence/0.009 goalie save percentage; hypothetical unsigned returns; or the Monday calendar described above. “Modelled injury penalties removed” does not erase injury effects already present in historical games played.

**Limits that matter.** No future trades, waiver acquisitions, individually forecast coaching changes, power-play promotions, NHL opponent-strength adjustments or individually forecast injury/recovery dates are included. Player development and ageing are represented only indirectly by recent rates and the scenario checks. The model does not fully reproduce player-by-player category correlations, NHL teammate correlations in actual scoring, redistribution of NHL goalie starts when a teammate is injured, real NHL outcomes shared across different fantasy owners, or managers choosing categories to target against a particular opponent. The last few regular-season points and low-probability championship prices should therefore not be treated as precise. Buffalo, Toronto, Pittsburgh, Vegas and other young teams are especially sensitive to individual prospects beating these generic assumptions.

The most useful next inputs are the actual twenty scoring-period boundaries and fixture list, any final-specific goalie minimum, the next tiebreak after equal regular-season points, and individualized prospect/goalie role projections. No further information is required to read this as a fixed-ownership baseline forecast.
